# StudySynth CTET — AI source gate for bundled chapters

Date: 2026-10-24
Status: awaiting approval

## Intent and why

The bundled CTET Library was generated by running the PWA study engine over
every page of each source PDF. The engine is good at finding evidence, but it
also builds questions from whatever prose it is handed, including running
heads, figure captions, timeline rows, exercise prompts, table-of-contents
spreads and narrative filler. The result is a large share of questions whose
"topic" is unusable — the learner opens a chapter and sees a question about
`years`, `we say`, or a whole paragraph.

Measured on three chapters (real engine runs, see Evidence below): **35%** of
generated questions had a usable topic over raw page text.

Fix #3 is about which source text the engine is allowed to generate from. The
constraint from the product owner: **questions are never reworded.** Every
evidence quote must remain the textbook's own words. So the lever is
*selection*, not rewriting.

## Goals

- Raise the share of bundled chapter questions whose topic is usable, without
  changing a single word of the source sentences used as evidence.
- Apply the gate to every source-derived set: **49 NCERT chapter sets + 9
  pedagogy chapter sets = 58 sets**. (An earlier count of "13 pedagogy" was a
  Library *group* figure — 9 chapters plus 4 solved-paper sets — not chapters.)
- Leave the 12 solved-paper sets and their 470 questions untouched.
- Keep the build reproducible: the gate's decisions are recorded data, not a
  step that re-runs an AI during the build.
- Make the quality/count trade-off explicit and measurable per chapter rather
  than hidden.

## Non-goals

- Rewording, summarising or otherwise rewriting any source sentence.
- Fixing the engine's question-topic wording. That is the larger defect
  (see Evidence) but the owner chose to defer it.
- OCR. Every source PDF has a real text layer.
- Rebuilding the Android package. Owner deferred.

## Anti-goals (this would be a failure even if it ships)

- A gate decision that drops a whole topic from a chapter (no questions on
  some part of the syllabus) without that being visible.
- A build that depends on network access, an AI provider, or a machine-local
  path that the recorded data does not carry.
- Shipping a bundle where a chapter's question count collapses silently.

## Constraints

- The engine contract is fixed: `analyzeSource(rawText, title, sourceMeta, N)`
  with `N = 25` for NCERT and `N = 30` for pedagogy.
- `output/`, `www/`, `node_modules/` and the Android assets are gitignored, so
  anything the build must reproduce has to live elsewhere in the repo.
- The service worker cache name must be bumped whenever a runtime asset
  changes, or the installed app serves stale content.
- Source PDFs live only on this machine (`C:\Users\Admin\Desktop\NCERT`). They
  are not in the repo, so no portable test may require them.
- Existing test contracts must keep passing: every `getElementById` in
  `pwa/app.js` exists in `pwa/index.html`; exactly one
  `aria-label="With love, usm007"`; no emoji in `pwa/index.html`;
  `data-mode="library"` is the default; every asset listed in `pwa/sw.js`
  exists on disk.

## Approaches considered

**A. Per-chapter AI source gate (chosen).** A chapter's page text is extracted
once, split into numbered candidate sentences with page furniture stripped,
and a separate AI pass returns the numbers worth generating from. The engine
then runs on **only those sentences, verbatim**. Spiked on three chapters; see
Evidence.

**B. Fix the engine's question-topic wording first.** The spike showed that
most of the *remaining* bad questions come from clean sentences: the routine
that decides what a question is "about" picks a long clause or a stray word.
This is deterministic, testable, and would lift every source including
documents a learner adds. **Deferred by the owner** — chosen to keep this
change to build inputs only.

**C. Do nothing.** Rejected: the measured 35% usable-topic rate is
user-visible.

Sub-choices inside A, presented to the owner:

- **Rule-based cleaning instead of an AI gate** — already measured as a dead
  end before this design: cleaning the page text left the usable-topic rate
  essentially unchanged (36% raw vs 35% cleaned). The judgement is not
  expressible as text-cleanup rules.
- **Gate every chapter inline during the build** — rejected: the build must not
  call an AI, and per-chapter coverage would then vary run to run.

## Evidence (spike, 3 chapters, real runs)

| chapter | sentences | kept | usable topics: before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class 6 — Locating Places on the Earth | 299 | 96 (32%) | 5/25 (20%) → 6/15 (40%) |
| Class 7 — Geographical Diversity of India | 262 | 116 (44%) | 15/25 (60%) → 19/25 (76%) |
| Class 8 — India's Long Road to Independence | 582 | 302 (52%) | 6/25 (24%) → 4/15 (27%) |
| **total** | 1143 | 514 (46% of text) | **26/75 (35%) → 29/55 (53%)** |

Usable-topic questions per 10,000 characters of source: **1.67 → 4.08**.

Two honest caveats that shape the design:

1. The *count* of usable questions barely moves (26 → 29), because less text
   yields fewer questions. The gain is precision per unit of source, not more
   questions.
2. Class 8 got slightly worse. The gate kept 52% of a story-heavy history
   chapter and the noise survived. Under the owner's choice below, Class 8 still
   takes the gated text, so its usable count will dip. This is a known,
   accepted outcome, not an oversight.

Why the remainder is still bad: of 88 questions the gated runs produced, 50 had
an unusable topic — 19 a whole clause, 13 a single stray word, 6 punctuation
inside the topic, 4 a leading preposition. Traced back, they came from clean
textbook sentences, e.g. "Each centimetre on the map represents a certain
distance on the ground … we say that the scale is 1 cm = 500 m." produced
topics `we say` and `= 500 m`; "Varāhamihira, a famous astronomer, lived and
worked there some 1,500 years ago." produced `years`.

## How it works (decided)

**Gate tool.** `scripts/ctet_source_gate.py` owns:

- extraction of a chapter's page text (whole PDF for NCERT; a page range for
  pedagogy),
- furniture stripping: page furniture, running heads (a short line that occurs
  more than once in the chapter's own text), figure/table/box captions, lone
  figure labels that carry a digit, table-of-contents spreads,
- sentence splitting, and rendering the numbered candidate list,
- rebuilding the gated text from the chosen numbers, in the original order,
  verbatim.

A chapter's candidate list is written to `output/ctet_gate/<id>.candidates.txt`
(regenerable, gitignored).

**Gate decisions are data.** The chosen numbers for each chapter are committed
at `data/ctet_gate/<id>.keep.txt`: one ascending integer per line, nothing
else. These files, not the AI, are what the build consumes. No source text is
committed: keep files carry almost no content, which keeps textbook text out of
the public repository.

**Audit record.** `data/ctet_gate/manifest.json` records, per set: the source
file name, the chapter title, a fingerprint of the prepared candidate text, how
many sentences were offered and how many were chosen, the resulting question
and revision counts, and which text the build used (`gated`). It is build-side
only; nothing from it is added to what the app downloads. Rebuilding still
requires the source PDFs on the build machine, exactly as the existing
converters already do.

**Wiring into generation.** Both chapter converters ask the gate for a
chapter's text:

- if `data/ctet_gate/<id>.keep.txt` exists, the engine runs on the gated text;
- if it is absent, the current raw-text behaviour is used unchanged.

**Trade-off, decided by the owner.** Gating a chapter keeps less source text, so
the engine returns fewer questions from it. The measured trade is fewer
questions at a markedly higher usable rate (per-10k-characters usable yield
rose 2.4×). The owner chose the gated text for **all 58 sets**, with no
automatic fallback to raw text: every chapter takes the gate's output even
where the usable *count* dips. Simplest rule, largest count reduction, and no
chapter silently keeps a stale input.

Because of that, the usability rule below is used for **measurement and
reporting**, not for choosing an input.

**Usable-topic rule.** Every quality number in this document comes from one
rule, which is now written into the project so the numbers are auditable: a
question counts as usable when the topic line the learner sees is a short
(`<= 45` characters), `<= 6`-word noun phrase with no punctuation inside it, no
verb or leading pronoun/determiner, no lone lowercase word, and no long number
string. This is a proxy for "would a learner understand what this question is
about", and it is deliberately strict.

**Reporting.** A measurement pass runs the engine over both raw and gated text
for all 58 sets and prints a per-chapter before/after table (usable count,
usable rate, total questions, revision inventory). It runs once during
implementation and the resulting table is reported; it is **not** part of the
build, and it never changes what ships.

**Review cost.** The spike validated one AI pass per chapter. Scaling means 58
gate passes. They will be dispatched in parallel batches of about six, each
chapter reviewed on its own candidate list, matching the validated spike. Say
the word if you would rather coarsen this (e.g. three chapters per pass) to cut
the number of passes at some cost in fidelity.

**Rebuild.** Regenerating the 58 cached modules cannot happen by re-running the
converters, which refuse to overwrite existing per-chapter output. The build
step adds an explicit force/only path so exactly the 58 gated sets are
regenerated, then publishes through the existing payload builder (index +
bodies + offline manifest), rebuilds `www`, and bumps the service-worker cache
name.

## What's left to the builder

- Exact `--force`/`--only` surface on the two converters, and the per-chapter
  report format.
- The gate's splitter behaviour on pedagogy text is unmeasured: it is coaching
  notes with heavy bullet lists. Expect to tune the furniture/turn splitting
  for it, and to review its candidate lists the same way.
- Batched dispatch mechanics for the 58 gate passes.
- The added test that keep files are well formed (ascending, unique, integers,
  one per gated set) and that the payload still publishes 70 sets.

## The CTET authoring profile

The gate rubric is the selection half of `profiles/CTET.json` from the
competitive-study-quiz project, and the brief now says so explicitly. That
profile describes a CTET item as source-bound, with one defensible key and
plausible distractors, and with no ornamental detail. Three of its rules are
usable here because the engine quotes the textbook rather than rewriting it:

- a tested claim must be traceable to the cited excerpt, which is why a sentence
  that only makes sense beside a figure or an earlier line should not be kept;
- a sentence must carry a single clean claim, since the engine derives
  distractors from the same typed facts;
- content survives, tone does not.

What the profile **cannot** deliver here is its situational archetypes:
classroom scenario, learner-error diagnosis, assertion-reason and passage
comprehension items all need authored stems and invented detail. Keeping a
sentence does not convert it into one of those forms, and this work never
rewords a stem or an option. Anyone reading the profile later should treat the
archetype list as out of scope for the gate, not as a promise the engine makes.

One profile setting conflicts with the shipped app and was left untouched: the
profile records CTET as having no negative marking
(`negative_marking.enabled: false`), while the app's exam mode scores
`+2.00 / -0.66`. Changing exam scoring is a product decision, not part of a
source-selection change.
